<Section n="01" id="problem" label="The problem" title="A program that existed, and didn't get used.">
<div class="prose">

The business goal was loyalty, using a points system the company already had. The problem was that people couldn't see the loop: you earned points depending on how you paid and whether the attendant remembered, and you redeemed them somewhere else, with a PDF catalog and a validation at the counter.

</div>

<Quote>No entiendo dónde canjear mis puntos.</Quote>
<Quote>¿Cómo acumulo puntos?</Quote>

<p class="dim" style="font-size:14.5px;margin-top:12px">Real quotes from the interviews, in Spanish: “I don't understand where to redeem my points”, “How do I earn points?”, “I can't see my points”.</p>
</Section>

<Section n="02" id="research" label="Research" title="20 interviews and a scope that fit in a month.">
<div class="prose">

I ran 20 interviews with drivers — as many as the timeline allowed — plus workshops with stakeholders, a pain-point map per journey and a heuristic evaluation of the existing app. The findings pointed to five problems: friction to earn, dependence on third parties, no earn → redeem loop, invisible benefits and low understanding of the system.

**What I cut.** The original brief included ordering gas cylinders. It was the least-used feature — people bought them directly at the station — so I took it out of the MVP to focus on earning and redeeming points.

</div>
<Insights>
<Insight n="P1" title="User autonomy">Earning shouldn't depend on the attendant. One scan at the pump or on a receipt.</Insight>
<Insight n="P2" title="A complete loop">Every earn screen points to what you can redeem; every reward shows how far you are.</Insight>
<Insight n="P3" title="A system people understand">Points are translated into things: “enough for a full car wash + 4 coffees”.</Insight>
</Insights>
</Section>

<Section n="07" id="visual" label="Visual design" title="Three directions, one system, two platforms." d="standard">
<div class="prose">

We explored three visual directions and chose one. For this portfolio I rebuilt the UI on **iOS 27 (Liquid Glass) and Android 17 (Material 3 Expressive)** with a variable-based foundation: primitives, semantic light and dark themes, platform tokens and spacing, plus a UI kit with iOS and Android versions of each component. The palette is new so the original brand isn't recognizable.

</div>
</Section>

<Section n="08" id="results" label="Outcome" title="Launched, without numbers to show.">
<div class="prose">

The MVP launched in 2023. I don't have metrics from the client, so I won't invent them. These are the numbers I'd watch: **share of fill-ups linked to an account**, **share of members who redeem at least once**, **time from first earn to first redeem**, and **support requests about points**.

</div>
</Section>

<Section n="09" id="ai" label="Appendix · personal exploration" title="An AI layer I explored, and why it didn't ship." d="standard">
<Note kind="ai" title="Not part of the product">

On my own, I explored natural-language search ("something for the kids under 500 points") and content that adapts to each driver's habits. I left it out: in a one-month MVP for a program people didn't understand yet, the priority was making the basic loop visible. Search, filters and rule-based recommendations covered the need with less risk.

</Note>
</Section>
