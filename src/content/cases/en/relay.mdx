<Section n="01" id="problem" label="The problem" title="Autonomy isn't one switch.">
<div class="prose">

Sending a summary to a Slack channel and updating a deal stage in the CRM are both "actions", but they carry very different risk. Relay starts from that: **autonomy is set per category of action**, it's visible to everyone it affects, and the agent always shows its plan before it acts.

I followed one request through the whole product — Priya, an ops manager, asks Relay to draft the Q3 update for the exec team and post it — plus one automation that hits a limit. Every screen below belongs to one of those two stories.

<Section n="07" id="tradeoffs" label="Key trade-offs" title="The tensions behind the design.">
<Decisions>
<Decision n="01" title="Policy by category of action, not by tool" tradeoff="Per-tool settings map to how IT thinks. Per-category settings map to risk, which is what admins and employees actually reason about.">
“Communications” covers email, Slack and DMs alike, because the risk is the same: a message goes out in someone's name.
</Decision>
<Decision n="02" title="A one-off approval and a rule change are separate choices" tradeoff="One button would be simpler. Keeping them apart stops a single exception from silently loosening the rule for everyone.">
The person can approve this email; raising the limit is an admin action with its own impact preview.
</Decision>
<Decision n="03" title="Show what was stopped, not only what was done" tradeoff="Hiding blocked actions makes the product look smoother. Showing them is what lets people believe the limits are real.">
Blocked actions appear on the home screen, in the audit log and in each person's trust center.
</Decision>
<Decision n="04" title="Preview the impact of a policy change before saving it" tradeoff="It adds a step for admins. It also turns “raise the limit” from a guess into a decision with names and amounts.">
The preview replays the last 30 days and flags accounts with open deals.
</Decision>
</Decisions>

<Note title="Open questions">

- Whether admins can set a sensible first policy without help, or need recommended presets by company size.
- Whether people read the plan before approving it, or approve by habit once it's familiar.
- Whether the trust center changes how much employees delegate to Relay, which is the real measure of trust.
