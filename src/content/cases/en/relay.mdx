<Section n="01" id="problem" label="The problem" title="Autonomy isn't one switch.">
<div class="prose">

Sending a summary to a Slack channel and updating a deal stage in the CRM are both "actions", but they carry very different risk. Relay's design starts from that: **autonomy is set per category of action**, it is visible to everyone it affects, and the agent always plans before it acts.

</div>
<Steps items={[["Set the policy","Admins choose per category: draft only, send with review, or full autonomy."],["Plan","For each request, Relay proposes its steps and marks the ones outside the policy."],["Approve and run","The person approves; execution is live and reversible steps are labelled."],["Audit","Every action, blocked or done, is logged with the policy that allowed it."]]} />
</Section>

<Section n="07" id="tradeoffs" label="Key trade-offs" title="The tensions behind the design.">
<Decisions>
<Decision n="01" title="Policy by category of action, not by tool" tradeoff="Per-tool settings map to how IT thinks. Per-category settings map to risk, which is what admins and employees actually reason about.">
“Communications” covers email, Slack and DMs alike, because the risk is the same: a message goes out in someone's name.
</Decision>
<Decision n="02" title="Blocked actions offer a one-off approval and a policy change, separately" tradeoff="One button would be simpler. Keeping them apart stops a single exception from silently loosening the rule for everyone.">
The admin can approve this email, or raise the threshold, but has to choose which.
</Decision>
</Decisions>
