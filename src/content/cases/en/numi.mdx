<Section n="01" id="idea" label="The idea" title="Plan from what's already there.">
<div class="prose">

Numi flips the usual order: you scan your grocery receipt (or tell Numi what you bought), it keeps a picture of your fridge, and it plans meals around that and around your day — a late meeting means a shorter dinner.

The risk in that idea is obvious. Reading a crumpled receipt or a photo of a shelf is error-prone, and a plan built on a wrong fridge is useless. So the design principle was **visible uncertainty**: Numi shows what it read, marks what it assumed and refuses to guess when it can't see.

</div>

<Section n="07" id="evaluation" label="Evaluation" title="I audited my own prototype before anyone else did.">
<div class="prose">

With the first-use flow working end to end (14 screens plus the error branch), I ran a heuristic evaluation against Nielsen's 10 heuristics and five UX laws. **24 observations: 13 meet the heuristic, 11 don't.** None is catastrophic; two are major, and both touch sensitive data.

</div>
<Table head={["Finding", "Heuristic", "Severity", "Change"]} rows={[["The receipt review was all or nothing", "Control and freedom", "<span class=\"sev sev--3\">Major</span>", "An editable review: fix one item without rejecting the whole receipt"], ["“None” could be selected together with allergies", "Error prevention", "<span class=\"sev sev--3\">Major</span>", "Restrictions in two groups, with exclusive “I don't have any” and “I eat everything”"], ["The camera didn't say whether to tap or wait", "Visibility of system status", "<span class=\"sev sev--2\">Minor · very frequent</span>", "Automatic capture with a countdown"], ["Onboarding asked for routine details, and “Skip” had no explanation", "Aesthetic and minimalist design", "<span class=\"sev sev--2\">Minor</span>", "A lighter routine step and a clear “Do it later”"]]} caption="The top three fixes were mocked up before testing. Strengths: it feels native to iOS, the system does the work (one receipt photo fills the fridge), and every step has a way out." />
<div class="prose">

**Then the AI itself.** I audited every AI moment against Microsoft's Human-AI interaction guidelines (HAX), using the 12 of 18 that apply to Numi: **5 met, 6 partially, 1 didn't** (G18, notify users about changes). The audit found things a usability review misses: the chat called itself a “nutrition assistant” when Numi deliberately avoids nutrition tracking; a message mentioned “your late meeting” from a calendar that was never connected; learning was invisible and impossible to reset; and swapping dinner silently used the chicken reserved for tomorrow's lunch.

</div>
<Steps items={[["Perceive", "Numi reads the receipt, photo or voice, and shows what it saw."], ["Propose", "It suggests a meal or a change. It never applies it silently."], ["Explain", "One line on why, and which data it used."], ["The person decides", "Accept, change or correct. Saying no costs the same as saying yes."], ["Learn", "Only after three matching signals, and visible in “What Numi knows”, where it can be forgotten."]]} />
<div class="prose">

That loop became a table of 10 behaviour rules for handoff, with four autonomy levels: the harder something is to undo, or the more sensitive, the less Numi does alone. Explicit feedback applies immediately; a declined permission isn't asked again for 30 days. It's an expert audit, not yet validated with users.

</div>
</Section>

<Section n="08" id="access" label="Accessibility and ethics" title="Who gets left out, and what pushes without meaning to.">
<div class="prose">

I reviewed three key screens in light and dark mode: I measured 62 text and background pairs with the WCAG formula, checked every touch target against 44 pt, and walked through Dynamic Type, VoiceOver and Reduce Transparency. The fixed colours already passed. The problems were in what a colour list doesn't measure.

</div>
<Table head={["Problem", "Before", "After"]} rows={[["The tab bar loses contrast when content scrolls under it", "3.53:1 over a photo", "6.76:1, and a solid bar when Reduce Transparency is on"], ["“That's not right” was the only way to correct an inferred fact", "A 63 × 17 pt text link", "A 44 pt secondary button"], ["Time-of-day labels in 11 pt capitals", "4.66:1, passing by 0.16", "13 pt, sentence case, 5.97:1"], ["The swap button was only an icon", "VoiceOver: “Change, button”, three times", "“Change dinner: pumpkin soup”"], ["Meal cards break with large text", "Titles cut off", "Cards stack at accessibility sizes"]]} />
<div class="prose">

I also crossed the persona with permanent, temporary and situational limits: one hand in a cast, wet hands while cooking, glare in the greengrocer's, a noisy supermarket, a 65-year-old cooking for her family. That's where a cooking mode came from: screen always on, one step at a time, “Next” by voice.

**No dark patterns, but pressure I hadn't noticed.** A message justified a lighter dinner “so you don't feel heavy”, which ties food to guilt. It now says “so you eat earlier and have more time to rest”. The goal “Eat healthier” promised no calorie counting, yet every card showed kcal; calories are now off by default. The streak had already become “active days this month”, which never resets.

</div>
</Section>

<Section n="10" id="tradeoffs" label="Key trade-offs" title="The tensions behind the design.">
<Decisions>
<Decision n="01" title="Refuse to read a bad receipt instead of returning a partial guess" ev="hypothesis" tradeoff="It's one more step for the user. A wrong item in the fridge silently ruins a week of plans.">
Below a confidence bar, Numi asks for another photo or method and explains why.
</Decision>
<Decision n="02" title="Label assumptions in place, not in a settings page" ev="hypothesis" tradeoff="Labels add noise to a summary screen. They're also the only way the user notices a wrong default before it shapes the plan.">
When the user doesn't mention something — like their schedule — Numi fills a sensible default and marks it “assumed” right there.
</Decision>
<Decision n="03" title="Active days instead of a streak" ev="hypothesis" tradeoff="A streak is a stronger habit hook. It also punishes exactly the tired days Numi exists to help with.">
“Active days this month” never resets, and it's never used in notifications.
</Decision>
<Decision n="04" title="Calories hidden by default" ev="hypothesis" tradeoff="Some people want the number. Showing it to everyone contradicts the promise of eating better without counting.">
Calories can be turned on in Profile for whoever wants them.
</Decision>
</Decisions>
