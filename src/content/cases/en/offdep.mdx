<Section n="01" id="problem" label="The problem" title="Buying for a company meant waiting for a sales rep.">
<div class="prose">

A buyer who needed supplies had to ask a rep for a quote and wait. Nothing in the old experience knew who belonged to which team, what budget they could spend or who had to approve.

The goal was to remove that dependency: **a self-service Business platform with role-based accounts**, using AI wherever it could save buyers and approvers time. The constraint I set: the AI could recommend, compare and prepare, but **every purchase and every approval stays a person's action**.

</div>
</Section>

<Section n="02" id="approach" label="Approach" title="Four ideas that shaped the platform.">
<Insights>
<Insight n="01" title="Self-service, not sales-dependent">Role-based Business accounts and permissions replace the sales-agent bottleneck, so login, checkout and reordering don't wait on a rep.</Insight>
<Insight n="02" title="An agent, not a chatbot">It learns each account's patterns and recommends inside the classic e-commerce flow people already know, instead of replacing it.</Insight>
<Insight n="03" title="Budget and policy, always visible">Live budget and policy checks surface before checkout and before approval, so nobody is surprised after the fact.</Insight>
<Insight n="04" title="Validated with real operators">Several rounds of prototype testing with real purchasing operators shaped decisions, from cart optimization to one-click approvals.</Insight>
</Insights>
</Section>

<Section n="07" id="tradeoffs" label="Key trade-offs" title="The tensions behind the design.">
<Decisions>
<Decision n="01" title="Keep the classic e-commerce flow and add AI inside it" ev="shipped" tradeoff="A chat-first experience would have been more novel. Buyers already knew how to shop; we put the AI where they were already looking.">
Catalog, cart and checkout work exactly as people expect. The AI shows up as labelled picks, savings cards and a verdict, never as a gate.
</Decision>
<Decision n="02" title="Savings are suggestions with the math, never silent swaps" ev="validated" tradeoff="Auto-applying every saving would maximize the number, but buyers would lose track of what's in their cart. Understanding comes first.">
Each suggestion shows the calculation and has a Not now. The approver sees the swap too, and the requester is notified either way.
</Decision>
<Decision n="03" title="Approve with changes as a first-class option" ev="validated" tradeoff="A third button adds a decision to a screen designed for speed. It removed the back-and-forth of rejecting an order to fix one item.">
Approvers can accept the AI's substitution, approve the original, or reject with a reason, without sending the order back.
</Decision>
</Decisions>
</Section>
