<Section n="01" id="problem" label="The problem" title="Speed wasn't the bottleneck. Trust was.">
<div class="prose">

Adjusters juggled many open claims at once. Their day started with the same work: open the queue, work out which claims were close to breaching their SLA, then copy policy numbers, VINs and dates from PDFs into forms. When a claim needed a supervisor, the evidence was spread across documents and tools.

The client wanted AI in the workflow. The risk was obvious from the first interviews: **an AI that fills in a claim wrongly is worse than no AI**, because an adjuster has to find the mistake before they can fix it. So the brief I wrote for myself was not "automate claims". It was: *let the AI do the reading and the preparing, and make every decision visibly a person's.*

</div>
</Section>

<Section n="02" id="research" label="Research" title="20 interviews across every role in the system.">
<div class="prose">

I led 20 interviews across the roles in the system, plus heuristic evaluations, desk research and a benchmark. I then tested prototypes with users before handoff. I summarised what we learned as four questions the product had to answer on every screen:

</div>
<Insights>
<Insight n="Q1" title="What should I work on first?">Adjusters lost the start of each day building their own priority list. → An AI-ranked queue with the reason for each rank.</Insight>
<Insight n="Q2" title="Can I trust what it filled in?">Nobody would accept autofilled fields they couldn't check. → Confidence and the source document on every value.</Insight>
<Insight n="Q3" title="What happens when it isn't sure?">Guessing was the failure people feared most. → Below a threshold, the AI leaves the field empty and asks.</Insight>
<Insight n="Q4" title="Who decided, and why?">Supervisors and auditors needed a trail. → Every AI action and every human override is logged with a reason.</Insight>
</Insights>
</Section>

<Section n="07" id="autonomy" label="Autonomy as a setting" title="How much the AI does alone is a decision the client makes, and can undo.">
<div class="prose">

The launched copilot shipped in **Draft for approval**: the AI prepares every action and nothing runs until a person approves. Instead of hard-coding that, I designed autonomy as a setting with four levels, a locked confidence threshold and a list of decisions no level can automate.

<Section n="09" id="tradeoffs" label="Key trade-offs" title="The tensions behind the design.">
<Decisions>
<Decision n="01" title="Leave fields empty instead of pre-filling a best guess" ev="shipped" tradeoff="It adds clicks on hard claims. We accepted that because a wrong pre-filled value costs more to find than an empty one costs to fill.">
A pre-filled value looks finished, and people skim past it. Below the threshold the field stays empty and the claim can't move on, so the uncertainty can't be ignored.
</Decision>
<Decision n="02" title="One threshold everyone can see, instead of hidden per-field tuning" ev="shipped" tradeoff="A single 85% rule is blunter than per-field models. Financial fields got a stricter 95% gate; everything else stayed simple enough to explain in one sentence.">
Adjusters and supervisors needed to predict when the AI would ask for help. A rule you can say out loud builds that faster than a smarter one nobody can explain.
</Decision>
<Decision n="03" title="Automation that the adjuster starts, with an undo window" ev="shipped" tradeoff="Fully automatic follow-ups would save more time. The human trigger keeps people in charge while trust builds, and the settings let the client raise autonomy later.">
Reminders to providers run from a "Ready to run" lane. Nothing reaches a claimant without a person reading it first.
</Decision>
<Decision n="04" title="Simulate before saving any autonomy change" ev="hypothesis" tradeoff="Designed for the portfolio extension; not validated with users.">
Raising autonomy is the riskiest change an admin can make. Showing its effect on the current queue turns a leap of faith into a decision with numbers.
</Decision>
</Decisions>
</Section>

<Metrics items={[{v:"−68%",l:"claim resolution time",note:"Client-reported, measured over 3 months. Claims that took weeks now close in days, some in hours."},{v:"90%+",l:"adoption among ~500 adjusters",note:"It replaced the previous tool. The rest were still in training at 2 months."}]} />
<div class="prose" data-d="standard">

**What I'd do differently.** I'd instrument overrides from day one. We knew adoption and resolution time, but not how often adjusters corrected the AI and why. That number would have told us where to raise autonomy next, which is exactly what the AI quality screen in the extension is for.
