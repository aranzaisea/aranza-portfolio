<Section n="01" id="problem" label="The problem" title="Agents can gather the evidence. They can't own the decision.">
<div class="prose">

An AML alert is a question: is this activity suspicious? Answering it means pulling transactions from core banking, checking sanctions and PEP lists, reading adverse media and writing it up. Most of that is collection, and collection is what agents are good at.

The design problem was the rest. In compliance, **a missing check that looks like a clear is worse than a slow check**, and a regulator will ask who decided. So three rules framed every screen:

</div>
<Insights>
<Insight n="01" title="Agents gather and check; they never close, file or sign.">The line is written on the inbox itself, so nobody has to remember it.</Insight>
<Insight n="02" title="A failed agent shows no result at all.">Never a partial score that could be read as an answer.</Insight>
<Insight n="03" title="Speed comes from batching, not from skipping.">Low-risk cases can be signed together, but only after the analyst reviews a random sample.</Insight>
</Insights>
</Section>

<Section n="04" id="batch" label="Batch sign-off" title="Sign 62 cases at once, after checking three.">
<div class="prose">

Most alerts are low risk and pre-cleared by a rule. Signing them one by one wastes the analyst's attention; signing them blind defeats the point. The batch flow asks the analyst to review a random sample first. If one sample looks wrong, the whole batch stops.

<Section n="05" id="tradeoffs" label="Key trade-offs" title="The tensions behind the design.">
<Decisions>
<Decision n="01" title="Fixed agent order instead of parallel runs" tradeoff="Running everything in parallel would be faster on good days. A fixed order makes every case read the same way and makes it obvious why an agent is waiting.">
Risk and Regulatory start only when Context has the transactions. If Context fails, they wait instead of producing results on incomplete data.
</Decision>
<Decision n="02" title="Ask one question instead of guessing an entity match" tradeoff="It adds a step for the analyst. A wrong “same entity” decision would change the outcome of the case.">
When a name is close to a list entry, the agent stops and asks the analyst one question, and the answer is recorded for future matches.
</Decision>
<Decision n="03" title="A flagged sample pauses the whole batch" tradeoff="One flag can delay 61 good cases. A rule that let one bad case through might be letting others through too.">
The analyst chooses what happens next, and the reason goes to the rule owner as structured data.
</Decision>
</Decisions>
</Section>

<Section n="06" id="results" label="Outcome" title="Launched, without numbers to show.">
<div class="prose">

Vantage launched in 2025. I don't have metrics from the client, so I won't invent them. If I were measuring it, I'd track **time from alert to decision**, **share of cases cleared by batch**, **how often samples are flagged** (a signal that a rule needs work) and **agent failure rate by source**.
