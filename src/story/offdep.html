01The problem

Buying for a company meant asking a sales rep and waiting.

A buyer who needed supplies had to request a quote and wait. Nothing in the old experience knew who belonged to which team, what budget they could spend or who had to approve. Login, checkout and reordering were full of friction. The client wanted Business accounts to become a retention strategy, with AI throughout.

02What I found

Buyers already knew how to shop. They didn't want a new way to buy.

I interviewed 20 Business buyers, and we tested the prototype with real purchasing operators over several rounds, from cart optimization to one-click approvals.

The AI could recommend, compare and prepare. Every purchase and every approval stays a person's action.

Self-service, not sales-dependentRole-based Business accounts and permissions replace the sales-rep bottleneck.
An agent, not a chatbotIt learns each account's patterns and recommends inside the e-commerce flow people already know.
Budget and policy, always visibleLive checks surface before checkout and before approval, so nobody is surprised later.
Savings people can checkEvery suggestion shows its math and can be skipped. Nothing changes until someone applies it.
03The key decision

Approvers can accept the AI's change without sending the order back.

Rejecting an order to fix one item meant a full round-trip between requester and approver. A third option adds a decision to a screen designed for speed, and it removed that back-and-forth.

What I chose
  • Keep the classic e-commerce flow and add AI inside it.Catalog, cart and checkout work as people expect. The AI shows up as labelled picks, savings and a verdict, never as a gate. Shipped
  • Savings are suggestions with the math, never silent swaps.Each one has a Not now, and the requester is notified either way. Validated
  • Approve with changes as a first-class option.Accept the AI's substitution, approve as submitted, or reject with a reason. Validated
What I discarded
  • A chat-first buying experienceMore novel, and it asked buyers to relearn something they already knew how to do.
  • Auto-applying every savingIt would maximize the number, and buyers would lose track of what's in their cart.
  • Approve or reject onlyFaster on paper, and one wrong item sent the whole order back.
04What I built

An AI that shows its math and says what it can't see.

The platform covers three roles: requesters who shop, approvers who decide and procurement leads who set budgets and policies. These are the AI moments that carry the rule. The UI is adapted to protect the confidentiality of the original product.
