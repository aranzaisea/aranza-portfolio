01The problem

The points program existed. Almost nobody used it.

The business goal was loyalty, using a points system the company already had. Earning depended on how you paid and whether the attendant remembered. Redeeming happened somewhere else, with a PDF catalog and a validation at the counter.

“No entiendo dónde canjear mis puntos.”I don't understand where to redeem my points.
“¿Cómo acumulo puntos?”How do I earn points?
“No veo mis puntos.”I can't see my points.

Real quotes from the interviews, in Spanish.

02What I found

People couldn't see the loop from earning to spending.

I ran 20 interviews with drivers, as many as the timeline allowed, plus workshops with stakeholders, a pain-point map per journey and a heuristic evaluation of the existing app. The findings pointed to five problems: friction to earn, dependence on third parties, no earn-to-redeem loop, invisible benefits and low understanding of the system.

A number of points means nothing until it turns into something you can get.

User autonomyEarning shouldn't depend on the attendant. One scan at the pump or on a receipt.
A complete loopEvery earn screen points to what you can redeem; every reward shows how far you are.
A system people understandPoints translated into things: “enough for a full car wash + 4 coffees”.
03The key decision

Cut what didn't serve the loop, and make earning one scan.

The original brief included ordering gas cylinders. It was the least-used feature, because people bought them directly at the station, so I took it out of the MVP. That left one month for the two things the interviews asked for: earning and redeeming points.

What I chose
  • One scan to earn, at the pump or on a receipt.Points no longer depend on how you pay or on the attendant. MVP
  • A catalog inside the app, and a code the attendant scans.Validation stays where it already happens, at the station. MVP
  • Two redeem flows for two kinds of reward.A coffee is a code on the spot; a product ships to a station, with tracking. MVP
What I discarded
  • Ordering gas cylindersIn the brief, and the least-used feature. People bought them at the station.
  • The PDF catalog and counter validationThe reason people didn't know where to spend their points.
  • An AI layer I explored on my ownNatural-language search and adaptive content. Search, filters and rule-based picks covered the need with less risk.
04What I built

A catalog in the app, and a code that works with bad signal.

The flows come from the 2023 MVP. For this portfolio I rebuilt the UI on iOS 27 and Android 17 with a variable-based foundation and a UI kit with both platform versions of each component. The palette is new, so the original brand isn't recognizable.

It launched in 2023. I don't have the numbers, so I won't invent them.

What I'd measure

The share of fill-ups linked to an account, the share of members who redeem at least once, the time from first earn to first redeem, and support requests about points.

Appendix · personal exploration, not in the product

On my own, I explored natural-language search (“something for the kids under 500 points”) and content that adapts to each driver's habits. In a one-month MVP for a program people didn't understand yet, making the basic loop visible came first.
