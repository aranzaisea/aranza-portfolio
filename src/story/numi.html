A meal planner that starts from your fridge, and tells you when it's guessing.

Numi plans what to eat around what you already bought, your schedule and your goals. I designed it end to end with one rule throughout: the AI says what it saw, what it assumed and what it won't guess.

01The problem

Meal planners assume you'll shop for their plan. Most people do it the other way round.

People buy groceries first and then wonder what to cook. Numi flips the usual order: you scan your receipt or tell it what you bought, it keeps a picture of your fridge, and it plans around that and around your day. A late meeting means a shorter dinner.

02What I found

A plan built on a wrong fridge is useless.

Reading a crumpled receipt or a photo of a shelf is error-prone. If Numi silently guesses what you bought, every plan after that is built on something you never had. That turned the core design problem from convenience into trust.

Numi shows what it read, marks what it assumed and refuses to guess when it can't see.

I framed the problem, sketched with Crazy 8s, mapped low-fidelity flows and compared three layouts for the Today screen before going to high fidelity. Every way of getting food in had to answer the same three questions.

03The key decision

Refuse a bad receipt instead of returning a partial guess.

Below a confidence bar, Numi asks for another photo or another method and explains why. It's one more step for the user. A wrong item in the fridge silently ruins a week of plans.

What I chose
  • Refuse below a confidence bar, and say why.One more step now beats a week of plans built on food you never bought. Hypothesis
  • Label assumptions in place.Labels add some noise to a summary, and they're the only way to notice a wrong default before it shapes the plan. Hypothesis
What I discarded
  • A partial guess from a bad photoIt looks helpful and quietly fills the fridge with mistakes.
  • Defaults hidden in a settings pageCleaner screens, and nobody finds the assumption until dinner is wrong.
04What I built

An assistant you can correct, at every step.

With a working prototype, I audited every AI moment against Microsoft's Human-AI interaction guidelines (HAX) and redesigned the screens that fell short, with clearer explanations and easier ways to correct the AI.

A complete, audited prototype. Not yet tested with users.
