An AI copilot that hands the hard calls back to people.

01The problem

Claims sat for weeks while people searched for what was blocking them.

Adjusters started every day building their own priority list, retyping data from PDFs into forms, and jumping between email, phone and the case. When a claim came back, nothing said why. Supervisors approved without the evidence in one place.

02What I found

Speed wasn't the bottleneck. Trust was.

I interviewed about 15 adjusters and 5 supervisors, ran a heuristic evaluation of the legacy tool, and benchmarked other claims platforms.

The job wasn't processing claims. It was searching for what blocked them.

The client wanted AI in the workflow, and the interviews made the risk clear: an AI that fills in a claim wrongly is worse than no AI. Four questions shaped everything after that.

What should I work on first?A queue ranked by the AI that shows the reason for each rank.
Can I trust what it filled in?Confidence and source on every value.
What happens when it isn't sure?It leaves the field empty and asks.
Who decided, and why?Every AI action and human override is logged with a reason.
03The key decision

Below 85% confidence, the AI stops guessing and a person decides.

Our first version only made information easier to read, and testing with 10 adjusters and 5 supervisors showed it saved little time. So the concept changed: from a dashboard to a workspace, with a copilot that finds problems on its own, and one rule everyone could repeat in a sentence.

What I chose
  • One visible threshold: 85%, and 95% for money.People can state the rule in one sentence. Shipped
  • Leave uncertain fields empty, with a one-line reason.A blank gets checked; fixing a wrong value costs more than filling a blank. Hypothesis
  • Automation the adjuster starts, with a 10-minute undo.Nothing reaches a claimant without a person reading it. Shipped
What I discarded
  • Pre-filled best guessesThey look finished, so people skim past them.
  • Per-field tuned modelsMore accurate on paper, and no one could explain them.
  • Fully automatic follow-upsFaster, and one wrong message to a claimant costs the trust we were building.
04What I built

Every AI suggestion arrives with its evidence.

The shipped rules, carried through the moments where they matter most. These screens extend the original product to show how each rule behaves in more states.

05What happened

Weeks became days, and the old tool was retired.

−68%claim resolution time, client-reported over 3 months. Some claims now close in hours.
90%+adoption among ~500 adjusters. The rest were still in training at 2 months.
1tool across the company. ClaimPro replaced the previous one.
What I'd do differently

Track overrides from day one: how often adjusters correct the AI, and why. That data shows where autonomy can safely grow next.
